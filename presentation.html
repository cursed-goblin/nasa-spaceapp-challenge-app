The Problem

Space missions must satisfy science, engineering, budget, and risk constraints simultaneously. Every choice opens one door and closes another.

  • Science objectives vary by target body (Earth, Moon, Mars, Venus, Europa, asteroid).
  • Each mission demands a different measurement suite, coverage, lifetime, and budget.
  • Engineers must balance mass, power, data, pointing, lifetime, and cost.

Split screen: Earth vs. Europa mission needs

What We Built

A browser‑based mission design game with a full design loop:

  • Briefing: choose mission objective and difficulty.
  • Spacecraft: select components across 7 subsystems; live mass/power/data/cost updates.
  • Orbit: compare 15 orbits for coverage, eclipse, radiation, comms.
  • Launch: test mass, fairing envelope, and programme cost across 6 vehicles.
  • Review: NASA‑MDL‑style compatibility check with JPL‑CML maturity badge.
  • Flight: deterministic 1800‑day simulation with live telemetry.
  • Report: score audit, science closure, findings, next design move.
  • Hangar: save runs, leaderboard, compare up to three runs side‑by‑side.

How It Works

Deterministic 1800‑day simulation with real‑time battery, data, power, and science telemetry.

  • Eclipse: power generation drops to zero during orbit eclipse.
  • Battery: state of charge tracked minute‑by‑minute.
  • Buffer overflow: data loss if storage exceeds capacity.
  • Downlink: data downlinked only during comms windows.
  • Degradation: hardware ages over mission lifetime.
  • Science return: measurements taken based on coverage and pointing.

NASA Data Used

TRADEOFF is built on open NASA resources:

  • CSLI CubeSat 101: mission‑design framing and launch‑vehicle sizing.
  • JPL CML framework: game‑adapted maturity badge (not an official assessment).
  • NASA mission‑viz: open‑source 3D orbit reference (deferred to SVG orrery).
  • GMAT: downloadable trajectory handoff scripts (structurally generated).

Impact & Reuse

Educators, students, and mission‑design teams can iterate designs instantly.

  • Zero backend: runs entirely in the browser, no account needed.
  • Shareable: URL‑encoded designs can be shared with a link.
  • Local hangar: save runs, compare up to three designs side‑by‑side.
  • Responsive: works on desktop, tablet, and mobile.
  • Open source: React 19, Vite 8, TypeScript, all MIT/Apache‑2.0 licensed.
